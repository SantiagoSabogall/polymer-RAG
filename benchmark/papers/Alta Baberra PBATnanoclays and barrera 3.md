# Antimicrobial, mechanical, barrier, and thermal properties of bio‐based poly (butylene adipate‐co‐terephthalate) (PBAT)/Ag2O nanocomposite films for packaging application

Bio‐based nanocomposites of poly (butylene adipate‐co‐terephthalate) (PBAT)/silver oxide (Ag2O) were prepared by the composite film casting method using chloroform as the solvent. The prepared Ag2O at different ratios (1, 3, 5, 7, and 10 wt%) is incorporated in the PBAT. The PBAT nanocomposite films were subjected to structural, thermal, mechanical, barrier, and antimicrobial properties. The electron micrographs indicated uniform distribution of Ag2O in the PBAT matrix. However, the images indicated agglomeration of Ag2O particles at 10 wt% loading. The thermal stability of the nanocomposite films increased with Ag2O content. The tensile strength and elongation of the composite films were found to be higher than those of PBAT and increased with Ag2O content up to 7 wt%. The PBAT‐based nanocomposite films showed the lower oxygen and water vapor permeability when compared to the PBAT film. Antimicrobial studies were performed against two food pathogenic bacteria, namely, Klebsiella pneumonia and Staphylococcus aureus.

#### 1 | INTRODUCTION

Poly (butylene adipate‐co‐terephthalate) (PBAT) biomaterial consumes several properties as a bio‐plastic, such as low cost, low density, high thermal stability, and degradable. Therefore, it is widely used in many applications, especially in food packaging. In composite flexible packaging materials, PBAT film is used mainly as the packed and the seal layer. This means that PBAT materials should not only have a high degree of mechanical toughness and barrier properties but also perform better in food safety. Nanocomposite is a multiphase solid material system that is formed by the combination of two or more components that include polymers and nanomaterials. Over the past decade, interest in biopolymer‐based nanocomposites has increased because of the biodegradability and improved physical, mechanical, and antimicrobial properties.1-3 In addition, remarkable antimicrobial functions showed by certain nanocomposites have expanded the application of nanocomposite films in various applications in the area of biomedicals and food packaging sectors. It is well known that silver ions and silver‐based compounds are highly reactive towards microorganisms4 showing strong biocide effects against many species of bacteria including Escherichia coli. Thus, silver ions, as an antibacterial

component, have been used in the formulation of dental resin composites,5 ion exchange fibers,6 and in coatings of medical devices.7 The possible use of silver nanoparticles as antibacterial agent has, therefore, been investigated as a means arresting increasing resistance bacteria.8

Use of different types of nanomaterials like copper, zinc,9 titanium,10 magnesium,11 gold,12 alginate,13 silica,14 and silver has increased and proved the most effective as they have good antimicrobial efficacy against a wide variety of bacteria, viruses, and other microorganisms. Poly (butylene adipate‐co‐terephthalate) is an aliphatic‐aromatic copolyester based on the monomers 1, 4‐butanediol, adipic acid, and terephthalic acid. Figure 1 shows the chemical structure of PBAT. It is a fully biodegradable polymer,15 and it is resistant to water showing good potential in its application for packaging films, compost bags, and agricultural films.16 The thermal and mechanochemical properties of PBAT/octadecylamine–modified montmorillonite nanocomposites have been studied.17 These results illustrated the property enhancement of PBAT nanocomposites prepared by a melt blending technique using montmorillonite (MMT). Cloisite 20A, Cloisite 30B, and Bentonite 10B nanoclays.18 The antimicrobial property of such nanocomposites makes it a versatile material in packaging

applications. The nanocomposites containing certain organically modified nanoclay offer strong antimicrobial function against both gram‐positive and gram‐negative bacteria.19 In that, the presence of quaternary ammonium groups present in the organically modified clays is responsible for the antimicrobial function of nanocomposite films. These nanocomposite films with enhanced mechanical and gas barrier properties have a high potential for being used as food packaging materials. The PBAT/polyvinyl alcohol–blended SiO2 nanocomposites show good antimicrobial activity against E coli and Staphylococcus aureus.20

In this article, the report shows the structural, mechanical, barrier, and antimicrobial properties of PBAT/silver oxide (Ag2O) nanocomposite film. The silver oxide nanoparticles (Ag2O NPs) were synthesized by chemical reduction method, and then the nanocomposite film was made by introducing Ag2O NPs into the PBAT by solution casting method. The morphology, mechanical, thermal, barrier, and antimicrobial activity of the prepared PBAT nanofilms were studied to find its suitability for food packaging applications.

- 2 | EXPERIMENTAL METHODS

- 2.1 | Materials

The PBAT was used as received from BASF Ltd, Japan. Trisodium citrate and silver nitrate were obtained from SRL, India. The test strains Klebsiella pneumoniae, ATCC‐13883, and S aureus, ATCC‐6538, were procured from IMTECH, Chandigarh. All chemicals and solvents were obtained from SRL, India.

- 2.2 | Preparation of silver oxide nanoparticles

The Ag2O NPs were prepared by sodium citrate reduction of AgNO3.21 After the addition of trisodium citrate (1% w/v) into AgNO3 solution, a dark brown precipitate was obtained. The precipitates were filtered and rinsed with distilled water, and the precipitate was heated in a tubular furnace at 200°C for 1 hour to obtain the Ag2O nanoparticles.

- 2.3 | Preparation of PBAT/Ag2O nanocomposite films

The polymer used in this study was previously dried at 60°C for 24 hours before use. A total of 2.0 g of PBAT polymer were dissolved in 100 mL of chloroform with constant magnetic stirring until the clear solution was obtained. Instantaneously, the prepared Ag2O NPs of the following wt% (1, 3, 5, 7, and 10) were dispersed in the polymer solution. The solution was cast into a petri dish and dried in an oven for 48 hours at 40°C. Finally, the resulting films were dried under vacuum for 48 hours to obtain the nanocomposites. 2.4 | Materials characterization

- 2.4.1 | Fourier‐transform infrared spectra analysis Fourier‐transform infrared (FT‐IR) spectrum of the film samples was measured using an FT‐IR spectroscopy (Perkin Elmer spectrophotometer RX1) operated at a resolution of 4 cm−1. Film samples were cut into rectangular shape (2 × 2 cm) and directly placed on the ray‐exposing stage. The spectrum was recorded at wavenumber of 400 to 4000 cm−1.
- 2.4.2 | X‐ray diffraction analysis

The crystal structures of the Ag2O NPs and nanocomposite film were studied by X‐ray diffraction (XRD) (Rigaku, MiniFlex 120 II‐C). Samples were prepared by placing rectangular shape of each film (2 × 2 cm) on a glass slide, and the spectra were recorded using Cu‐Kα radiation. The size of the nanoparticles was calculated through the Scherer's equation.

where d, K, λ, β, and θ are the average crystallite (nm), constant factor, X‐ray wavelength, full width at half height, and scattering angle, respectively.

- 2.4.3 | Scanning electron microscopy Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) analysis was performed to observe the microstructure of the film samples. A small piece of film was mounted on a SEM specimen holder and analyzed using a SEM (S‐4800, Hitachi Co, Ltd, Japan) with an accelerating voltage of 5.0 kV.
- 2.4.4 | Transmission electron microscopy

The morphology of Ag2O NPs and nanocomposite film was examined by transmission electron microscopy (TEM) using a microscope TECNAI‐G2 (model T‐30), operated at an accelerating voltage of 300 kV.

- 2.4.5 | Thermogravimetric analysis The thermal stability evaluation was performed using a thermogravimetric analyzer model QA 50 (TA Instruments). The samples were heated from 25°C to 700°C at the rate 10°C min−1 under nitrogen atmosphere (50 mL min−1).
- 2.4.6 | Mechanical properties The mechanical properties of PBAT and nanocomposite films, viz, tensile strength and percentage elongation, have been done. The tests for measurement of these mechanical properties were done using Universal Testing Machine (UTM, H10KS, Tinius Olsen, UK),

- 2.4.7 | Oxygen transmission rate The oxygen transmission rate of the PBAT and nanocomposite films was calculated by oxygen permeability tester (Noselab ats, Italia) at 25°C under the condition of 0% relative humidity at 1 atm by the standard of ASTM D‐3985. The measurements were taken at 3 times at different places of the film, and the average value was calculated. All specimens were conditioned at ambient conditions.
- 2.4.8 | Water vapor transmission rate The ASTM E‐987 standard method was used to determine water vapor permeability (WVP). The samples were kept at 25°C and 50% RH in a conditioned chamber for 2 hours. The weight loss of each cup was considered to be equal to the water transferred through the film and adsorbed by the desiccant. Weight loss from each cup was measured as a function of time for 12 hours. The WVP was calculated by

WVP ¼ WVTR × x=Δp

where WVTR is the water vapor transmission rate (g m−2 per 24 h) through the film, x is the film thickness (m), and Δp is the partial water vapor pressure difference.

- 2.4.9 | Antimicrobial activity

The antimicrobial activity of PBAT and PBAT/Ag2O nanocomposite films was tested by an inhibition zone as per ASTM D‐2149 standard method. Food pathogenic bacteria, K pneumonia and S aureus, were used for finding the antimicrobial activity of the films. For the qualitative measurement of antimicrobial activity, a small piece of film samples were punched to make disks (diameter = 11 mm), and the antimicrobial activity was determined using a modified agar diffusion assay. The plates were kept for 2 days after incubation at 37°C for possible clear zones.

3 | RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

- 3.1 | Fourier‐transform infrared spectra analysis

The characterization of the prepared Ag2O NPs was analyzed using FT‐ IR. Absorbance bands at 3441, 1658, 1535, and 1400 cm−1 were indicative of the stretching vibrations of primary and secondary amines (Figure 2). The result revealed that the capping ligand of the Ag2O NPs may be an aromatic compound or amines. The absorption peak at 1040 cm−1 represents the Ag–O; this bond was confirmed the Ag2O NPs. The evaluation of antibiotic resistance of pathogenic fungi has stimulated the search for effective antifungal agent from alternative sources. Many studies have shown the antimicrobial effects of Ag2O NPs.

Figure 3 shows the FT‐IR of the PBAT with different loadings of Ag2O nanoparticles. The FT‐IR was performed to study the effects of functional groups in the Ag2O NPs with respect to potential interactions with the polymer components. - 3.2 | X‐ray diffraction analysis

The XRD patterns of the Ag2O NPs prepared by the chemical precipitation method at 100°C are shown in Figure 4. The diffraction peaks are well indexed to the Ag2O structure. The intensity of the peak increases with increased crystallinity of the Ag2O NPs. The Ag2O showed 2 characteristic peaks at 27.9° and 32.2°, which correspond to the crystal plane 110 and 111, of Ag2O nanoparticles. The diffraction peaks at 46.3°, 54.9°, and 67.4° can be indexed to 211 and 222 planes of fcc Ag2O. The size of the nanoparticles was thus determined to be about 50 ± 2 nm for Ag2O NPs synthesized at 50°C.

The XRD showed the crystal structure of PBAT and PBAT/Ag2O nanocomposite films, and the XRD spectra are depicted in Figure 5. The PBAT showed 5 diffraction peaks of the crystal structure at 17.3°, 20.4°, 23.0°, and 23.9°, at 2θ value. These 5 characteristic peaks of pure PBAT were also observed for PBAT/nisin samples at the same 2θ values (Figure 5). Nevertheless, small changes in the intensity of the polymer diffraction peak were detected for the PBAT/Ag2O samples, suggesting that the peak intensity tends to decrease when Ag2O was added to PBAT matrix (5 to 10 wt% Ag2O). As the Ag2O content was increased from 0 to 10 wt%, the 2θ value of the peak increased from 17.3° to 23.9°. The XRD results indicate that the introduction of Ag2O enhanced molecular arrangement in the crystalline and structures polymer.

- 3.6 | Oxygen transmission rate

Nanocomposite materials that prevent oxygen permeation are being studied for use in packaging industries. The PBAT/Ag2O nanocomposites are the class of biodegradable material of bio origin suitable for manufacturing of packaging films. The oxygen transmission rate of Ag2O‐based nanocomposite PBAT films was determined, and the results are shown in Table 4. The oxygen permeability ranged from 1150.5 to 300.2 cc m−2 per day, depending on the driving force used and on the nanoparticle content, whereas the control recorded 1150 cc m−2 per day. The PBAT and Ag2O nanoparticles concentration influences oxygen permeability significantly. It was also observed that the maximum reduction in oxygen permeability over the control was 76% for the Ag2O 10%. A pronounced decrease in permeability with the higher driving force is observed. It reduces about 76% of its initial value at 10% Ag2O content. It was observed that oxygen permeability decreased with the increase in Ag2O percent.

- 3.7 | Water vapor transmission rate

The WVTR is one of the most important parameters for biodegradable films. The WVTR to the pure PBAT and nanocomposites with different concentrations of Ag2O is shown in Table 4. By adding the nanoparticles and increasing the film thickness, the WVTR of the film decreased.

Most of the reduction in gas penetration was observed in the Ag2O concentration of 10%. In such treatment, the WVTR of the film decreased by 29%. The water vapor penetration reduction is due to incorporation of nanoparticles because it would result in a more complicated path for oxygen gases to penetrate. Ray et al25 reported similar results for water vapor permeation after incorporation of nanoparticles. Moreover, the results of this study confirmed by increasing the Ag2O content; the WVTR shows a reduction.

#### 4 | CONCLUSIONS

In summary, the PBAT‐based nanocomposites were prepared by solution casting method using Ag2O as a nanofiller. The studies of FT‐IR show the formation of nano polymer matrix of PBAT, and the results of SEM image analysis reveal the morphological nature of the prepared films. It is found that the addition of Ag2O nanoparticles in the films improves the mechanical strength and shows good antimicrobial properties. The tensile strength of the prepared nanocomposite films was increased from 30.60 to 47.70 MPa by increasing weight percentage of nanoparticles in the film from 1 to 7 wt%. The film becomes stiff

- at higher loading of 10 wt% of Ag2O nanoparticles. The increase in filler loading shows good oxygen permeability from 1150 to 300 cc m−2 per 24 hours and also effective antimicrobial properties. Based on the results, it is found that increase in nanofiller wt% improves mechanical, oxygen permeability, and antimicrobial properties but at a higher loading of PA‐10 film, becomes stiff. Hence, it is concluded that the prepared film with 7 wt% of nanofiller with PBAT can be selected as a suitable film to find application for suitable food packaging applications.
